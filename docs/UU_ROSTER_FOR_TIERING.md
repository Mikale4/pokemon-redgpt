# RedGPT — the OU → UU pass: which OU entries are underwhelming?

**Purpose.** A briefing for an AI assistant deciding **which Pokémon currently
in RedGPT's OU are not pulling their weight and should drop to UnderUsed.**

It is the downward mirror of the Ubers pass run on 2026-08-16, and it is
deliberately self-contained: **the movepool data ChatGPT asked for last time is
included up front** (§4 and §6), along with every move whose numbers differ from
Showdown (§3).

Everything here was generated from the live packaged data and the real damage
engine on **2026-08-16**. None of it is written from memory. Where this file
disagrees with your knowledge of Smogon or the main series, **this file is right
for this game.**

---

## 1. The question

RedGPT imports Smogon's National Dex tiers and corrects them where its own rules
change the answer. Two passes have run so far, both recorded below. This one
asks the opposite question from the last:

> **Given how RedGPT actually plays, which of the 93 OU entries are weak enough
> that OU is not the right home for them?**

What "underwhelming" means here, and what it does not:

- **Not "low BST".** OU contains Clodsire at 430 and Toxapex at 495. A wall's
  job is not measured in stat totals.
- **Not "I have not seen it used".** There is no usage data for RedGPT and you
  must not invent any.
- **It is "does this entry do a job that OU needs, better than something already
  in UU does it?"** A Pokémon that is outclassed at its own role by a UU entry
  is the clearest possible drop candidate, and §6 gives you both rosters'
  movepools side by side so you can make exactly that comparison.

A tier *format* is cumulative downward: the OU pool is 602 legal species, the UU
pool 529. So dropping an entry from OU to UU does **not** remove it from OU play
— it stays legal there, it just stops being one of OU's own. What changes is
that it becomes legal in UU, and UU has to be able to take it.

### Behavioural changes that do not show up as numbers

These are engine rules, so the packaged data looks untouched. They matter at
least as much as the base powers above.

**Five priority moves are unconditional.** They keep their priority and lost
their activation conditions entirely:

| Move | Priority | What was removed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sucker Punch** | +1 | no longer needs the target to be attacking (and 80 BP, up from 70) |
| **Thunderclap** | +1 | no longer needs the target to be attacking |
| **First Impression** | +2 | usable on **any** turn, not just the user's first |
| **Upper Hand** | +3 | no longer needs the target to be using priority, and no longer flinches |
| **Grassy Glide** | **+1 with no terrain requirement** | the packaged move still reads priority 0 — **the +1 is applied by the engine**, so do not judge it from move data |

**Fake Out deliberately keeps its first-turn rule.** It shares a code path with
First Impression and was left alone on purpose.

**Conditions the *opponent* controlled were removed** — the test applied was
"can the user do anything to satisfy this?":

- **Dream Eater** no longer needs a sleeping target, and keeps its drain.
- **Poltergeist** no longer needs the target to hold an item.
- **Thief / Magician / Pickpocket** steal into an occupied item slot, destroying
  what the thief held. **Covet deliberately keeps** the restriction.
- **Fling** actually consumes the item now, and applies its status effect.
- **Bug Bite, Pluck, Stuff Cheeks, Tea Time** genuinely apply a Berry's effect
  (Stuff Cheeks and Tea Time previously consumed the Berry for nothing).

**Five moves take their type from the user at use time** and were all broken
until 2026-08-14: Revelation Dance, Judgment, Multi-Attack, Techno Blast, Aura
Wheel. Revelation Dance and Tera Blast also take their **category** from the
user's better attacking stat.

**Tera Blast is dead in OU.** It needs Terastallization, which OU does not have.
Treat it as an unusable move slot.

**Things you might expect to be changed and are not.** Checked explicitly
because they are natural assumptions: **Hyper Beam and Giga Impact are exactly
upstream** — 150 BP, 90% accurate, and they still require a recharge turn.
Accuracy, PP and secondary rates track upstream everywhere except the two named
redesigns in the table above.

## 4. RedGPT mechanics that decide a downward call

The upward pass cared about ceilings. A downward pass cares about floors, and
these are the rules that raise or lower one.

1. **Mega Evolution is free.** No Key Stone; holding the species' own stone is
   enough, and it is on in every format. **28 of the 93 OU entries are Megas**,
   and **23 of the 91 UU entries are too.** The cost is the item slot — a Mega
   set has no Life Orb, no Choice item, no Ring Target, no Leftovers.
2. **No Terastallization in OU.** The format rule is off, not merely unused.
   Anything whose case rests on a defensive Tera or on Tera-granted coverage is
   weaker here than you expect — this is the single most common reason a
   Generation 9 Pokémon underperforms in RedGPT.
3. **Unconditional priority suppresses frail attackers.** With Sucker Punch at
   80 BP with no condition, and Upper Hand at +3, a fast glass cannon is much
   easier to remove than on Showdown. That is a *downward* pressure on frail
   sweepers and an *upward* one on bulky priority users.
4. **Ring Target** turns any single type immunity into neutral damage, one per
   team, visible at Team Preview, and it occupies the item slot. It rescues a
   mono-attacker that a single immunity would otherwise wall — relevant when
   judging whether a narrow Pokémon is really walled.
5. **Several abilities are stronger than upstream**: Parental Bond's second hit
   is 50%, Transistor 1.5x, the `-ate` family 1.3x, Gale Wings works at any HP,
   Magic Guard also ignores Toxic Spikes and full paralysis, and **Intrepid
   Sword / Dauntless Shield fire on every entry**, not once per battle.
6. **Protean and Libero grant STAB again** — fixed 2026-08-16. Until that day
   the ability retyped the user and gave **no** offensive STAB, which made
   Greninja, Cinderace and Meowscarada substantially weaker than they read. All
   three are OU entries and all three just got a real 1.5x on every off-type
   move. **Do not judge them on pre-fix impressions.**
7. **Supreme Overlord is a flat +1 Attack on entry**, with no fallen-ally
   scaling — Kingambit is better early and worse late than you expect.
8. **Eleven species keep pre-nerf base stats.** In OU that means **Zamazenta**
   (92/**130**/115/80/115/138) and **Ting-Lu** (**165**/110/**130**/50/80/45).
   Aegislash (150/150 defences) and Cresselia are in UU and RU respectively.
9. **Toxic and Flame Orb activate on entry**, so a Guts or Poison Heal holder is
   boosted on the turn it arrives rather than a turn later.
10. **Every species keeps every ability it has ever had.** Gengar has Levitate,
    the Litwick line has Shadow Tag, Zapdos has Lightning Rod.
11. **A forme reached by an ability can be banned without banning the Pokémon**
    (new, 2026-08-16). Zygarde-10% plays in RU but may not run Power Construct
    there, because Zygarde-Complete is Ubers. This applies only where the base
    has another usable ability.

## 6. Movepools — both rosters

This is what was missing last time. Every entry in OU and in UU, with the data a
tiering call actually turns on: best STAB, real coverage, setup, recovery,
priority, hazards, removal, pivoting and utility.

**How to read it:**

- Base powers are **RedGPT's**, so they already include every change in §3.
- `P` / `S` marks physical or special.
- `25x2-5` is a multi-hit move: flat 25 BP per hit, two to five hits. Ranked on
  its *average* hit count, not its maximum.
- **Coverage is filtered to the category the Pokémon can actually attack from.**
  A legal physical move on a 40-Attack special attacker is not coverage and is
  not listed. Both categories are shown when Attack and Special Attack are
  within 20 of each other.
- **STAB is filtered the same way, but falls back and says so** — a
  `**(wrong category)**` tag means its only STAB of that type is on the wrong
  side of its stat spread, which is itself a finding.
- `**none**` is meaningful: no setup, no recovery, or no coverage at 70+ BP.
